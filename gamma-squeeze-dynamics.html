Gamma Squeeze & Market Maker Hedging Dynamics

Published: August 2026 • 10 min read • By ColdBloodedTraders

While spot and perpetual swap order books dictate daily micro-structure, options market positioning commands the macro gravitational pull of modern assets. Understanding how dealer gamma exposure forces automated, aggressive buying and selling gives advanced traders an unmatched predictive edge.

"Market makers do not take directional bets; they manage risk. When options flows force their hedging hand, price acceleration becomes a mathematical certainty rather than a choice."

1. The Mechanics of Dealer Delta Hedging

When market makers (dealers) sell call options to retail or institutional investors, they take on short gamma and short call exposure. To remain neutral and avoid directional risk, dealers must buy the underlying asset as its price rises (and sell as it falls). This reflexive feedback loop is known as delta hedging.

2. Positive vs. Negative Gamma Regimes

The market's structural volatility relies heavily on whether dealers sit in a positive or negative gamma environment. In positive gamma, dealers stabilize price action by fading moves. In negative gamma, their mechanical hedging acts as gasoline, amplifying breakouts into violent squeezes.

Gamma Regime Dealer Hedging Action Market Volatility Impact Price Tendency
Positive Gamma Sells rallies, buys dips Suppressed / Mean-reverting Range-bound
Negative Gamma Buys rallies, sells dips Amplified / Explosive Trend continuation / Squeeze

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a gamma squeeze?

A gamma squeeze occurs when heavy retail or institutional call option buying forces market makers to buy the underlying asset aggressively to maintain a delta-neutral hedge as the price rises.

Why do market makers hedge their exposure?

Market makers maintain delta-neutral books to isolate themselves from directional price risk, meaning they must automatically buy when prices go up and sell when prices go down.
