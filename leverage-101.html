Leverage 101: How Crypto Leverage Actually Works

Published: August 2026 • 8 min read • By ColdBloodedTraders

Leverage is often misunderstood as merely a tool to boost potential profits. In reality, leverage is capital efficiency optimization—allowing a trader to control a larger market exposure with a smaller upfront capital requirement (margin).

"Leverage does not alter your risk profile; position size does. High leverage simply moves your liquidation threshold closer to your entry."

1. Initial Margin vs. Maintenance Margin

To open a leveraged position, an exchange requires two distinct margin thresholds: Initial Margin and Maintenance Margin.

Concept Definition
Initial Margin Minimum collateral required to open a trade
Maintenance Margin Minimum equity required to keep the position open

If your equity falls below the Maintenance Margin due to adverse price movement, the exchange initiates a liquidation process to close the position automatically.

2. Managing Liquidation Risks

High leverage severely compresses the distance between your entry price and your liquidation price. Understanding how to calculate your liquidation threshold accurately using tools like our Liquidation Price Calculator is mandatory before deploying capital.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between initial margin and maintenance margin?

Initial margin is the minimum capital required to open a leveraged position, whereas maintenance margin is the minimum equity required to keep that position open before a liquidation is triggered.

Does high leverage increase my trading risk?

High leverage itself does not change market risk per trade, but it moves your liquidation threshold much closer to your entry, reducing your margin for error during volatile price swings.
